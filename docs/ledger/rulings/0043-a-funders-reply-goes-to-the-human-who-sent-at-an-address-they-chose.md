## The ruling

Ruling 0042 settled that a funder's reply goes to the person who sent, at an
address they choose. Its first clause also said a reply must never go to "the
platform's own, unattended, sending address", and its test of compliance
turned that into a refusal: a chosen address equal to the sending address was
not used. That clause assumed the sending address would always be platform
infrastructure nobody reads. It is not always: an organization may send as one
of its own people's real mailboxes, and the owner of this platform has done
exactly that — the address funders see the email come from is the same
mailbox they reply to. Under 0042 as written that choice would be silently
ignored and replies would go to whichever address the person signs in with,
which is the very outcome the ruling exists to prevent. This ruling removes
the refusal and keeps everything else. Four things hold.

1. **A reply goes to the person who sent, at an address that person
   controls and chose.** The address is the email of the account they are
   signed in with, or another address they typed themselves. It is never an
   address supplied by anyone else — not a model, an import, a funder-side
   source or another user. *(0042 clause 1, without the sending-address
   exclusion.)*

2. **The sender may choose which of their own addresses that is,** per
   person, defaulting to their account email, clearable, shape-checked, stored
   with the person and not for the organization or the platform. The platform
   does not pretend to verify that the person controls it, because the only
   replies it can misdirect are their own. It may equal the address the email
   is sent from: when it does, the sender is writing from and replying to the
   same mailbox, which is a valid and common arrangement, and the platform
   neither refuses it nor quietly substitutes another address. *(0042 clause 2,
   extended to say so.)*

3. **What is shown is what is sent, from one source,** captured on the
   attempt at its birth, before the provider is called (ruling 0029 clause 2).
   Changing the choice afterwards changes no record of anything already sent.
   *(0042 clause 3, unchanged.)*

4. **The sending address is configured by the platform owner,** and this
   ruling concerns only where replies go. It changes neither who may send
   (ruling 0032), nor the one send path (ruling 0029). *(0042 clause 4,
   unchanged.)*

## Why

A guard written against a failure that cannot occur here caused one that can:
it would override the person's explicit, correct choice and send their funders'
replies to an agency inbox. The guard protected a reply from landing in an
unattended mailbox; the same protection comes, without the refusal, from the
address only ever being the person's own — never one the platform supplies —
and from the confirmation showing the exact address before every send.

## Test of compliance

Set a reply address equal to the sending address: it is used, the confirmation
shows it, and the attempt records it. Set another address of your own: used.
Clear it: the sign-in address. Enter something that is not a single plausible
address: refused. A second person's choice never changes the first person's
replies. No reply address is ever one the platform chose for the person.

## Scope

Authorizes the amendment to item 106: remove the sending-address exclusion
from the reply-address resolver and from the settings control, and update the
assertions that pinned it. Does not authorize any change to the sending address,
the provider call, the attempt ledger, who may send, or a per-organization
sending identity (item 58).
