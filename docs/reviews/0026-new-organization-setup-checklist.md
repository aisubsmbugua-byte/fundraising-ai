# Setting up your organization: what to have ready

A checklist for beta testers. Everything here is something you type, choose or upload in the platform. Nothing has to be perfect on day one, but the AI can only be as specific as what you tell it. Allow roughly an hour for the profile if you have the material to hand (our estimate).

## The short version

- You do not fill in a long form up front. The platform owner creates your organization, you get an email invitation, and you sign in with an emailed link. There is no password.
- **Nothing on your organization profile is strictly required.** But two features will not run until you have saved a profile (matching you to the right kinds of funder, and searching for new funders), and every AI suggestion gets better the more specific your profile is.
- You will meet three kinds of boxes: required (marked below), recommended (the AI uses it a lot), and optional.
- Anything you type into a free-text box may be shared with AI services. Section 7 says exactly what is shared and what to keep out.
- Your first screen after signing in is an empty pipeline. Go to the Organization page first. It opens ready to edit.

## 1. What the platform owner needs from you to start

| What | Notes |
|---|---|
| Your organization's name | The platform owner types it once to create your account. You will type your own public name again on your profile; the two are not linked. |
| Email address of the first person | They receive the invitation. Use an address they actually read. |
| Names and email addresses of teammates | You can invite them yourselves later (Section 6), but it helps to know who. |
| Whether you want sending from the platform | Switched on per organization by the platform owner. It is off until they do. |
| Whether you want the AI Assistant | Also switched on per organization by the platform owner. Optional. |

## 2. Your organization profile

This is the most important page. It is your organization's own knowledge base, and every AI feature reads it: matching funder types, finding funders, strategies, email drafts, proposals and follow-up suggestions. Specific beats general. The Settings page shows a completeness ring (17 items) so you can see how far along you are.

## Identity

| What we ask | What to write, and why it matters |
|---|---|
| Organization name (**recommended**) | Your public name. It appears as the sender name on emails to funders and at the top of proposals. Sending is refused without it. |
| Legal / tax status (optional) | Choose one: 501(c)(3) Public Charity; 501(c)(3) Private Foundation; Fiscally Sponsored; Church / Religious Organization; Other. If Other, describe it. |
| Year founded (optional) | A number, such as 2014. |
| Website (optional) | For example yourorg.org (https:// is added for you). Shown on your profile only; it is not sent to AI. |
| Social media links (optional) | The platform name and the link, as many as you like. Shown only; not sent to AI. |

## Leadership

| What we ask | What to write, and why it matters |
|---|---|
| Leadership: founder, executive director, board chair, etc. (**recommended**) | For each person: full name (needed to add them), title or role, and phone (optional). Names and roles help the AI write in a real voice. A phone number you enter can be placed in email signatures and is shared with the AI, so add one only if you are comfortable with both. |

## Money

| What we ask | What to write, and why it matters |
|---|---|
| Annual operating budget in US dollars (**recommended**) | A number. A dollar sign and commas are fine. |
| Current funding need or gap (**recommended**) | One or two sentences, for example: $150k general operating gap for FY26. |

## Mission and focus

| What we ask | What to write, and why it matters |
|---|---|
| Problem statement (**recommended**) | What problem do you exist to solve? |
| Mission statement (**recommended**) | Your mission, in your own words. |
| Vision statement (optional) | The future you are working toward. |
| Programs (**recommended**) | What you actually do: programs, services, activities. |
| Cause areas (**recommended**) | Tick any that apply: Education; Health and Wellness; Human Services; Environment and Conservation; Arts, Culture and Humanities; Economic and Community Development; Human and Civil Rights; Youth Development; Disaster Relief and Public Safety; International and Foreign Affairs; Christian Faith Based; Animal Welfare. There is also an "Other cause area" box. |

## Who you serve and where

| What we ask | What to write, and why it matters |
|---|---|
| Population(s) served (**recommended**) | Who you serve. This is the "who we serve" statement the AI uses everywhere, so be concrete about who they are and what they face. |
| Geographic area served (**recommended**) | Pick tags such as Nationwide, International or any US state, or type your own wording. |
| HQ location (optional) | City and state. |

## Values and track record

| What we ask | What to write, and why it matters |
|---|---|
| Core values or guiding principles (optional) | One per line: faith tradition, community values, operating philosophy, whatever applies. |
| Key outcomes and impact numbers (**recommended**) | One per line, with real numbers where you have them. Funder-facing drafts are also told to ground claims in your verified evidence (Section 3), so keep these accurate. |
| Notable past or current funders (optional) | The funder's name and its city and state. The platform uses these to look for similar funders. |

## Look and feel, and documents

| What we ask | What to write, and why it matters |
|---|---|
| Logo (optional) | PNG, JPEG, SVG or WebP, up to 5 MB. It appears at the top of proposals. Anyone who has the link to the logo can view it. |
| Primary and accent colors (optional) | Six-digit hex codes such as #0b1f3a. Left blank, the default navy and teal are used. Used on proposal documents only. |
| Documents (optional) | 990s, strategic plans, annual reports. PDF, Word, Excel, CSV or text, up to 25 MB each. Private to your team. They are stored for people to read; the AI does not read them. |

## 3. Evidence: your proof

Evidence is verified outcomes, stories and testimonials your team is happy to put in front of a funder. **The AI only ever uses evidence that someone on your team has verified and approved.** Nothing else reaches a funder-facing draft.

| What we ask | What to write, and why it matters |
|---|---|
| Title (**required**) | A short name for the item. |
| Type (**required**) | Outcome, Story, Testimonial or Document. |
| Description (**required**) | Write it as you would want a funder to read it. The AI reads the title, type and description (and program and geography when drafting proposals and decks). |
| Program (optional) | Which program it belongs to. |
| Geography (optional) | Where it happened. |
| Linked document (optional) | One of the documents you uploaded. |

How approval works: every new item starts as **Needs review**. Someone on your team clicks Verify and then decides: **Verify and approve for AI** (safe to cite to a funder) or **Verify and restrict** (keep it internal). Start with your strongest handful of items.

## 4. Funders and people you already know (all optional)

You can start with an empty pipeline and let the platform find funders, or bring what you already have.

## Funders you are already talking to

Add one at a time under Prospects, or import a list.

| What we ask | What to write |
|---|---|
| Name (**required**) | The funder's name. |
| Channel (**required**) | What kind of funder it is: Foundation and Family Trust; Regranting Ministry; Christian Business; Denomination and Network Fund; Individual Church; Donor-Advised Fund (DAF); Major Donor. |
| Organization, Contact name | If different from the name, and who you deal with. |
| Contact email | Optional, but **needed before you can send an email to this funder.** |
| Website | Include https://. |
| Legal name and EIN | The legal name if different, and the nine-digit EIN such as 62-6041468. It helps the platform identify the right organization when researching. |
| Notes | Anything useful. |
| Where are they today? | Discovery (the default), Outreach, Proposal, Decision, Awarding or Stewardship. If you start anywhere but Discovery, give a short reason. |
| Ask amount, Next action, Next action due | What you are asking for, the next step, and its date. |
| Funder intelligence (optional) | Location, funder type, geographic focus, typical grant size, focus areas (comma-separated). Fill in only what you already know. |

**Importing funders from a file (CSV):** required columns are `name` and `channel`. The channel must be exactly one of these lowercase words: `foundation`, `regranting`, `christian_business`, `denomination`, `church`, `daf`, `major_donor`. Optional columns: `organization`, `website`, `contact_name`, `contact_email`, `location`, `funder_type`, `geographic_focus`, `typical_grant_size`, `focus_areas`. Rows with a missing name or an unrecognized channel are skipped and counted. Duplicates are skipped and counted separately.

**A funder's past giving to you:** import with columns `prospect_email` or `prospect_name`, `amount`, `gift_date` (such as 2026-03-15) and optional `note`. Gifts are matched to funders you already have; it never creates new ones.

## Individual donors (supporters)

For people who give small or recurring gifts. They are kept separate from funders you court.

| What we ask | What to write |
|---|---|
| Name (**required**) | The donor's name. |
| Email, Phone | Optional. |
| How they arrived | Event, Website or Other. Plus a detail such as which event. |
| Pledged amount and frequency | Optional. One-time, Monthly or Annual. The pledge decides how often the platform reminds you to stay in touch. |
| Notes | Optional. |

**Importing donors from a file:** required column `name`. Optional: `email`, `phone`, `source_type` (event, website or other), `source_detail`, `pledged_amount`, `pledged_frequency` (one_time, monthly or annual), `notes`. A column headed "Projected Annual Amount" (or Annual Amount, Annual Pledge, and the Monthly versions) is recognized and sets the frequency for you. Dollar signs and commas are fine in pledge amounts.

**Importing giving history:** columns `supporter_email` or `supporter_name` (it must match exactly one donor you already have), `amount` as a plain number, `gift_date`, and optional `note`. **Gift records cannot be edited or deleted once saved.** A correction is a new entry.

## People you know (your network)

This is how the platform suggests who could introduce you to a funder.

| What we ask | What to write |
|---|---|
| Name (**required**) | The person's name. |
| How close are you (**required**) | Close, Warm or Acquaintance. You must choose; there is no default. |
| Where they work or serve | Their organization. |
| How you know them | A short sentence. |
| Notes | Optional. Keep sensitive details out. |

**Importing from any list:** a donor list, a board list or any export works. You pick which column means what on screen, choose one closeness for the whole batch, and can import up to 5,000 people at a time. Email, phone, address and social-link columns are never imported or stored, and the screen tells you which columns were left out.

## 5. Your pathway to a grant giver

Grant givers rarely say yes to a stranger. The fastest door is usually someone you already know who knows someone there. The platform can suggest those paths for you, but only from people you tell it about and facts about the funder that you have checked and approved. The two worksheets at the end of this PDF are for gathering that before your first session.

**Worksheet A: people you know who could open a door.** Think of:

- Board members, and the friends and colleagues they could introduce
- Major donors and long-time supporters
- Pastors, church leaders and denomination or network leaders
- Partner organizations and coalition members
- Volunteers and alumni
- Former employers and colleagues of your team
- Local business and civic leaders
- People who work for, or sit on the board of, organizations that a funder has already given to

For each person write their name, where they work or serve, their role, how you know them, and how close you are. A rule of thumb: Close means they would take your call today; Warm means they know you well enough to make an introduction; Acquaintance means you have met a few times.

**Tip that makes the biggest difference:** in the notes, write the grant giver's name and the link, for example "Knows the board chair at the Smith Foundation". The platform only suggests a path when your entry and an approved fact about the funder genuinely connect: the same organization, the same church or body, or a relationship you have written down. The more specific you are, the better it works.

**Worksheet B: contacts at the grant giver.** For each grant giver you are pursuing, write the organization, the person to reach and their role, an email address, who could introduce you, and any notes. The platform keeps one contact name and email for each funder; extra names go in the notes.

**Getting the list into the platform.** Type people in under Network, or put Worksheet A into a spreadsheet and import it as a CSV file. A ready-made template is included: columns `Name`, `Organization`, `Role or title`, `How you know them`, `Notes`. You choose which column means what, pick one closeness for the whole batch, and the platform never stores email addresses, phone numbers or social links for the people in your network. A funder's own contact email is the only contact detail kept, and it lives on the funder's record.

What you write about these people is shared with an AI service when paths are looked for (Section 7), so keep sensitive details out of the notes.

## 6. Optional settings

| Setting | What it is |
|---|---|
| Team | Invite teammates by email. Everyone on the team has full access; there are no separate roles. An invitee cannot already belong to another organization on the platform. |
| Reply address | Under Settings, choose where funders' replies go. The default is your sign-in email. Use an address you read, such as your organization's. |
| Sending email to funders | Off until the platform owner switches it on for you. It needs your organization name on the profile, an approved intro-email draft, the funder's contact email, a subject and a body. Emails go out as plain text under your organization's name, and **nothing sends without your click on a confirmation screen** that shows exactly what will be sent. A sent email cannot be unsent. |
| Screening rules | None exist by default. Optional rules score a funder as Strong fit, Possible or Unlikely: a label, a description, a channel (or all), an importance (Nice to have, Important or Critical), and a test such as "organization contains church". |
| Overnight auto-search | Off by default. You can switch it on from the Donor Finder page. |
| AI Assistant and connectors | Optional; switched on by the platform owner. |

## 7. What is shared with AI services, and what to keep out

The platform uses AI services to do the research and drafting. Here is what leaves the platform.

| When | What is sent |
|---|---|
| Matching funder types, finding funders, strategies, email drafts, proposals, follow-up suggestions (Anthropic's Claude) | Your profile as you wrote it: name, legal status, year founded, budget, funding need, problem, mission, vision, programs, cause areas, who you serve, geography, HQ, values, outcomes, notable funders, and your leadership names, roles and phone numbers. For a particular funder it also sends that funder's details (name, organization, website, channel, location), the contact's name and email when drafting an email, the last few notes you logged when suggesting a next step, and your approved evidence. |
| Looking for introductions through your network (Claude) | The name, affiliation, how-you-know-them text and notes of the people being considered, plus approved facts about the funder. |
| The AI Assistant (Meta's Muse Spark), only if switched on | What you type and look up: names, stages, dates and research facts. If you ask about a strategy or draft, its full text. Never email addresses, phone numbers, notes or the text of logged interactions. |
| Never sent | Your website and social links, logo, colors, uploaded documents, individual donors and gift records. |

**Keep out of free-text boxes:** sensitive personal details about individuals (health, immigration status, family circumstances) and anything you would not want an AI service to see. Free-text notes are the one place the platform cannot police. The AI providers' own data-retention terms are theirs and the platform does not control them.

## What happens on your first day

1. You receive an invitation email and click the link. There is no password.
2. You land on an empty pipeline.
3. Go to the Organization page. It opens in edit mode because there is no profile yet.
4. Fill in the profile, add your logo, then add your first evidence items.
5. Add or import any funders and donors you already have.
6. Use the completeness ring on the Settings page to see what is left.

There is no forced checklist. You can work in any order and come back to improve anything later.

If something here is unclear, ask the person who invited you.
