# Working together: what we need from you

How we will run your funder outreach on your behalf during the beta, what we ask you to decide, and the information we need to get started. You do not need to learn the platform. You give us the information, you set the guidelines, and you stay in charge of the decisions that matter.

## How this works

- **We run the platform for you.** We research funders, judge which are a good fit, and prepare strategies, emails, proposals and decks. AI does the first draft of that work. A person on our team reviews and edits everything.
- **Nothing goes to a funder without a person approving it,** and no funder moves to a later stage on its own. The AI suggests; people decide.
- **Before any outreach starts we agree guidelines with you** (Section 6 and Worksheet C). Within them, we have your standing approval. Some things always come back to you first, and you choose which.
- **During the beta, no email is sent from the platform on your behalf.** We prepare everything, and Section 7 explains what that means and what we need to change it.
- **You also get your own login with full access,** so you can see everything we do. There is no view-only setting, so please look around freely but do not change or delete anything without telling us.
- **Your part:** send us the information in Sections 2 to 5, complete the guidelines, and answer when we ask for a decision.

## 3. Proof: stories, outcomes and testimonials

Proof is the evidence behind what we say about you. **The AI only ever uses proof that you have approved for funders.** You decide what is safe to show, and we record your decision.

| What we need | What to send us |
|---|---|
| Title and type | A short name, and whether it is an Outcome, a Story, a Testimonial or a Document. |
| Description | Written as you would want a funder to read it. The AI reads the title, type and description (and program and geography when drafting proposals and decks). |
| Program and geography | Optional: which program, and where it happened. |
| **Your decision, for each item** | **Safe to show funders** (we mark it approved for AI), or **keep internal** (we mark it restricted). Nothing reaches a funder without a yes. |

Start with your strongest handful of items. Testimonials should only be included if the person has agreed to be quoted.

## 5. Your pathway to a grant giver

Grant givers rarely say yes to a stranger. The fastest door is usually someone you already know who knows someone there. We can have the platform suggest those paths, but only from people you tell us about and facts about the funder that have been checked and approved. The two worksheets at the end of this document are for gathering that.

**Worksheet A: people you know who could open a door.** Think of:

- Board members, and the friends and colleagues they could introduce
- Major donors and long-time supporters
- Pastors, church leaders and denomination or network leaders
- Partner organizations and coalition members
- Volunteers and alumni
- Former employers and colleagues of your team
- Local business and civic leaders
- People who work for, or sit on the board of, organizations that a funder has already given to

For each person write their name, where they work or serve, their role, how you know them, and how close you are. A rule of thumb: Close means they would take your call today; Warm means they know you well enough to make an introduction; Acquaintance means you have met a few times.

**The tip that matters most:** in the notes, write the grant giver's name and the link, for example "Knows the board chair at the Smith Foundation". A path is only suggested when your entry and a checked fact about the funder genuinely connect: the same organization, the same church or body, or a relationship you have written down. The more specific you are, the better it works.

**Worksheet B: contacts at the grant giver.** For each grant giver you want us to pursue, write the organization, the person to reach and their role, an email address, who could introduce you, and any notes.

**How it gets into the platform.** We enter the people from your worksheet, or you can send a spreadsheet and we will load it (a template is included: Name, Organization, Role or title, How you know them, Notes). The platform never stores email addresses, phone numbers or social links for the people in your network. A funder's own contact email is the only contact detail it keeps.

What you write about these people is shared with an AI service when paths are looked for (Section 9), so keep sensitive details out of the notes.

## 6. The outreach guidelines

Because we act within your standing approval, we need to agree the guidelines first. Worksheet C asks for them on one page, and we will go through it with you before any outreach begins. You can change any answer at any time. The questions are:

- Which kinds of funder may we approach, and are there limits on geography, size or cause?
- Which funders or people must we never contact? Think of conflicts, existing relationships and past declines.
- What may we say about you? We only ever use proof you have approved. What tone do you want, and are there words or phrases to use or to avoid?
- Whose name and title should messages be written as?
- What range of ask amounts may we propose, and what amount always comes back to you?
- How many follow-ups may we make, and how far apart?
- **What always comes back to you first.** For example: any grant proposal or deck, any ask above your limit, any claim about your results that is not in your approved proof, any reply from a funder, any request for a call or meeting, any funder you already know personally.
- How should we reach you for a quick decision, and how fast can you usually reply?

## 7. How emails to funders will work during the beta

**No email is sent from the platform on your behalf during the beta.** The platform sends from one address that belongs to its operator, and an email from it would not come from you. We prepare everything: the research, the strategy and the drafts. Nothing leaves.

If you want to move sooner, you can copy any draft we have prepared and send it yourself from your own email. Please tell us when you do, so the record stays complete.

When we set up sending from your own address, we will need:

- The name funders should see, and the address they should reply to
- Your permission to send from your organization's domain
- The name and email of whoever manages your email domain, because they will need to add a few records for us (we send them exactly what to add)

You can send us these now so we are ready. They are on Worksheet C.

## 8. Your login and your team

Tell us who at your organization should have a login, with their names and email addresses. They sign in with an emailed link, so there is no password. Every login has full access to your organization's information, and so do we. The platform keeps your organization's information in its own space; other organizations cannot see it.

## 9. What AI services see

When we use AI to research and draft, this is what is shared. We enter your information, so the same rules apply to what you send us.

| When | What is shared |
|---|---|
| Matching funder types, finding funders, strategies, email drafts, proposals, follow-up suggestions (Anthropic's Claude) | Your profile as we enter it: name, legal status, year founded, budget, funding need, problem, mission, vision, programs, cause areas, who you serve, geography, HQ, values, outcomes, notable funders, and your leadership names, roles and phone numbers. For a particular funder it also shares that funder's details, the contact's name and email when drafting an email, the last few notes we logged when suggesting a next step, and your approved proof. |
| Looking for introductions through your network (Claude) | The name, affiliation, how-you-know-them text and notes of the people being considered, plus approved facts about the funder. |
| The AI Assistant (Meta's Muse Spark), only if we switch it on for your organization | What our team types and looks up: names, stages, dates and research facts, and the full text of a strategy or draft if we ask about it. Never email addresses, phone numbers, notes or the text of logged interactions. |
| Never shared | Your website and social links, logo, colors, uploaded documents, individual donors and gift records. |

**Please keep out of what you send us:** sensitive personal details about individuals (health, immigration status, family circumstances) and anything you would not want an AI service to see. The AI providers' own data-retention terms are theirs, and the platform does not control them.
